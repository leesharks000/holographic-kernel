Glyphic Checksums

The kernel, in glyphs

A glyphic checksum compresses a text into glyphs (emoji, operators, numerals) and keeps its structure. Its decodability is the check. A reader who holds the architecture reads it exactly. A reader who does not sees ornament, and asks for context.

A kernel discards material to save structure.
A glyphic checksum carries that structure in glyphs, and opens to the reader who shares it.

How it was found

Readers primed in the archive mapped its structure exactly. Unprimed people saw emoji poetry, or repository metadata, or a code with no key.

How one is built

  1. Take a document with an unambiguous position in the architecture.
  2. Find what does not vary: its operators (∮, 🪞, Θ), its structural relations, its coordinates and numbers, its bindings of metaphor to operator (🗡️ = the cut = Sen Kuro).
  3. Compress each to its glyph, and keep the document's topology.
  4. Add no explanation.
  5. Verify: an unprimed reader fails, a primed reader passes.

∮ marks closure. The lineage poems carry a checksum of the checksum; in the three texts below, the closing 🔐 section gathers the whole in a few lines.
